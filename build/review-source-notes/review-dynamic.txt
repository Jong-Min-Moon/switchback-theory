READING RECORD: DYNAMIC / MARKOV / PARTIAL OBSERVABILITY PAPERS
2026-10-15. Read local PDFs via pdftotext -layout. Scope: abstract, introduction,
related work, discussion/conclusion. Narrow lookups of model/assumption statements,
design inputs and estimand/CLT centering only; no proof deep dive. Page references

1. HU AND WAGER
Exact local first page: Yuchen Hu and Stefan Wager, "Switchback Experiments under
Geometric Mixing". arXiv:2209.00197v4 [stat.ME], 13 Dec 2025; Draft December 2025.
Scope: BOTH design/tuning and analysis; temporal carryover, with whole-system
treatment used to avoid cross-unit spillover rather than separately estimating a
spatial interference graph. Causal estimation (GATE, filtered ATE), conservative
asymptotic confidence intervals, not primarily sharp-null randomization testing.
Read: introduction/related work pp1-4, discussion p21; narrow model pp4-7,
tuning pp10,13, CLT statements pp11-15.

Model/assumptions (verbatim short statements and exact equations):
Assumption 1 p4: "For all time points t = 1, ..., T, the evolution of the underlying
state S_t is governed by a pair of (deterministic) state transition distributions"
P_t^0, P_t^1 with S_{t+1}|F_t,Y_t ~ P_t^{W_t}(.|S_t). Also Y_t=mu_t(S_t,W_t)+epsilon_t,
mutually independent noises, E[epsilon_t|F_t]=0 and Var[epsilon_t|F_t]<=sigma^2.
Assumption 2 p5: "There exists a mixing time t_mix < infinity such that, for all
time points t = 1, ..., T and w in {0,1},"
  ||f'P_t^w-fP_t^w||_TV <= exp(-1/t_mix)||f'-f||_TV
"for any pair of distributions f and f' on S_t."
The finite horizon t_mix is a contraction scale, NOT an exact last nonzero lag.
Past treatment effects may persist forever but contract exponentially. The
time-specific transition/reward functions may vary arbitrarily; slow/persistent
effects that accumulate are excluded by uniform contraction (p5 explanation).

Known values / nuisance distinction:
Assumption 2 is an existence statement and does not itself say t_mix is known.
The rate-guaranteeing tuning in Corollary3 p10 uses b=(t_mix/2)log T, l=b+C1;
the bias-corrected GATE tuning likewise depends on t_mix (p13).
Thus implementing those particular guarantees requires a defensible mixing-scale
bound or tuning rule; the estimators themselves require neither observing states
nor fitting transition kernels. The paper explicitly emphasizes this in related
work p3. Simulations p20 use domain knowledge such as typical ride duration to
choose block length conservatively, not an asserted known latent state model.

Important version-specific author claims:
Naive DM without burn-in has best worst-case RMSE O(T^-1/3) for GATE in their
nonstationary model. Burn-in DM estimates a filtered average treatment effect,
which excludes selected periods; it may differ from GATE under arbitrary temporal
heterogeneity. Corollary3 p10 obtains near T^-1/2 RMSE for ordinary burn-in DM
against GATE ONLY IF FATE-GATE=O(sqrt(log T/T)). Bias-corrected weighted estimator
can target full GATE at near-parametric rate. Discussion p21 explicitly contrasts
changing target to FATE with using bias correction to retain GATE.
Reviewer's assessment: a known exact m is replaced by a uniform quantitative decay
condition, not eliminated dependence knowledge. A short ride-duration statistic
need not certify mixing of all latent operational and behavioral states. Do not
write that ordinary burn-in always fixes GATE estimation or that geometric mixing
is empirically verified simply because no state is required.

2. HU (SPECTRAL)
Yuchen Hu, "Spectral Design of Random-Duration Switchbacks". Columbia University;
arXiv:2609.13698v1 [stat.ME], 12 Sep 2026; Draft September 2026.
Scope: BOTH (design-centered with HT error analysis); temporal only. Estimates
finite-horizon continuous-time global average effect. MSE/minimax design,
not a supplied confidence-interval or sharp-null testing theory in the reviewed
front matter. Read intro/related pp1-4; no standalone conclusion, last main
paragraph p18; narrow assumption p6 and Algorithm1 p14.

Assumption 1 (Finite carryover and non-anticipation), p6, verbatim:
"There is a carryover horizon m > 0 such that, for any two assignment paths w
and w',"
  w(s)=w'(s) for every s in [t-m,t] => Y_t(w)=Y_t(w').
Assumption2 p7 bounded pure outcomes |Y_t(1)|<=B, |Y_t(0)|<=B a.e. t.
Assumption3 p9 requires absolute integrability of the design covariance kernels.
Potential outcomes fixed, arbitrary temporal variation; no Markovian state needed.
Known values: exact m appears in pure-exposure eligibility, exposure probability
p_G=E[(R-m)_+]/(2E[R]), and design Algorithm1 p14 explicitly says "Input: Carryover
horizon m > 0 and experimentation horizon T > 0." Hence chosen m is an actual
analysis/design input, not merely an existence constant. Can conservatively use
a valid upper bound, at the cost of fewer pure exposure observations.
Authors: stationary alternating-renewal assignment lets run durations be design
primitive; two-rate age-dependent switching lowers asymptotic worst-case MSE by
at least 32% relative to regular Bernoulli fixed-block switchback, within specified
criterion/classes. End p18: randomization without lattice alone helps little;
run-age-dependent hazard yields gains across tested outcome frequencies.
Reviewer's assessment: this attacks timing regularity, not unknown/nonzero-tail
carryover; no finite m learning or sensitivity guarantee should be attributed.

3. JIA, KALLUS AND YU
Su Jia, Nathan Kallus, Christina Lee Yu, "Clustered Switchback Designs for
Experimentation Under Spatio-temporal Interference". arXiv:2312.15574v5 [math.ST],
26 Mar 2025. Cornell University.
Scope: BOTH; temporal AND network/spatial interference jointly. Causal estimation
of all-units/all-times GATE, finite-sample MSE guarantees; introduction does not
promise CIs or sharp-null testing. Read intro/related pp1-4 and discussion 3.2 p9;
narrow setup/assumptions pp5-7 and tuning p9. No final standalone conclusion.

Assumption1 (Rapid Mixing), p6, verbatim:
"There exists a constant t_mix > 0 such that for any i in U, t in [T],
w in {0,1}^{N(i)} and distributions f, f' over S, we have"
  d_TV(f P_it^w, f' P_it^w) <= e^(-1/t_mix) d_TV(f,f').
The dependence of each individual's next state on that individual's own current
state and treatments of its graph neighbors is an exact local structure. State
evolutions are independent across individuals conditional on treatments (pp3,5).
States need not be observed, p5 explicitly: "We emphasize that we do not assume
observation of the state variables."
Assumption2 p6: conditionally mean-zero uncorrelated noises across individuals and
times, variance bounded sigma^2. Assumption3 (delta-FNE) p6: for a in {0,1} and
w s.t. ||w-a1||_1 <= delta|N(i)|, mu_it^w=mu_it^{a1} and P_it^w=P_it^{a1}.
delta=0 recovers exact neighborhood interference; positive delta assumes invariance
to a specified small fraction of discordant neighbor treatments (not decay).
Known values: interference graph/neighborhoods and chosen clustering/exposure rule
are used for exposure probability weighting. t_mix's assumption is existence,
but Theorem1 p9 chooses l=r=t_mix log(NT), requiring an operational mixing bound
to invoke that concrete rate. delta is part of specified exposure model; it cannot
be treated as learned automatically. The temporal truncation is deliberately
misspecified relative to infinite memory; mixing bounds its bias.
Authors claim near 1/(NT) MSE up to logs for good sparse/geometric clusterings;
the graph-dependent exposure factor is crucial. Discussion p9 notes fixed block
length can be externally imposed and r can be tuned separately.
Reviewer's assessment: separately useful extension beyond temporal switchback;
knowledge burden includes graph and contraction scale. Conditional cross-unit state
independence rules out many network propagation mechanisms even though assignments
spill over through neighbors; do not describe as fully general interacting MDP.

4. WEN ET AL.
Qianglin Wen, Chengchun Shi, Ying Yang, Niansheng Tang, Hongtu Zhu,
"Unraveling the Interplay between Carryover Effects and Reward Autocorrelations in
Switchback Experiments". ICML 2025, PMLR267. Local first page has no arXiv stamp.
Scope: BOTH (comparing/tuning designs for several existing RL estimators);
temporal only (city-wide treatment; spatial randomization explicitly different).
Finite-day cumulative all-treatment/all-control ATE; estimation/MSE primary,
bootstrap CIs studied empirically and discussion relates efficiency to testing power.
Read intro/related pp1-3, discussion/impact pp9-10; narrow setup pp3-6,
assumption AppendixD pp22-23 only to copy regularity.

Model p4 (verbatim key conditional assumption):
  Pr(S_{t+1}=s'|A_t,S_t,{S_j,A_j,R_j}_{j<t})=p_t(s'|A_t,S_t),
  E(R_t|A_t=a,S_t=s,{S_j,A_j}_{j<t})=r_t(a,s).
Residual e_t=R_t-r_t(A_t,S_t) can be temporally correlated. State features S_t
are observed; trajectories (days) are assumed i.i.d., allowing within-day time
inhomogeneity p_t,r_t. This is not Hu-Wager arbitrary single-trajectory setting.
No exact cutoff m: their m denotes assigned treatment duration, not assumed
carryover horizon. Carryover-strength parameter p6:
  delta=max_{s,t} sum_{s'} |p_t(s'|1,s)-p_t(s'|0,s)|.
Do not confuse delta with decay speed: it is treatment-induced kernel difference.
For the OLS model p5, equation(1):
  r_t(A_t,S_t)=alpha_t+S_t' beta_t+gamma_t A_t,
  E(S_{t+1}|A_t,S_t)=phi_t+Phi_t S_t+Gamma_t A_t.
Assumption4 p22, verbatim: "For any 1 <= t <= T, ||Phi_t||_2 <= rho_Phi for
some constant 0 < rho_Phi < 1." This is model-specific contraction/stability;
not a finite carryover window. Different estimator results use subsets of
regularity assumptions. AppendixD Assumption6: transition functions uniformly
bounded away from zero and infinity. It would be inaccurate to impose linearity
on every estimator: LSTD/DRL allow nonlinear approximations under their conditions.
Known values: algorithm estimates OLS coefficients, value functions and density
ratios. rho_Phi and delta appear as theoretical constants/conditions, not explicitly
known prior inputs. Workflow p9 asks practitioners to discretize until Markov
condition reasonable, assess carryover magnitude, then residual correlation;
requires credible data/structural assessment, not a supplied exact cutoff.
Authors: with small carryover and positive residual autocorrelation, frequent
switches can improve efficiency across RL estimators; strong carryover or negative
correlation favors less switching/alternating day. Uncorrelated errors yields
asymptotic equivalence under weak carryover. Impact statement warns of significant
carryover, complex spatial interactions, high switch costs, rapidly changing systems.
Reviewer's assessment: useful demonstration that "more frequent always worse"
is false when estimators model dynamics, but results depend on observability,
between-day independence and correct/adequate nuisance estimation. No universal
best switch rate should be inferred from residual correlation alone.

5. BLANCHET ET AL.
Jose Blanchet, Peter W. Glynn, Ramesh Johari, Linjia Wu, Wenqian Xing,
"Conservative Inference in Switchback Experiments". AISTATS2026, PMLR300,
Tangier, Morocco (first-page publication line).
Scope: ANALYSIS-centered; fixed alternating design with implications for length/
washout choice rather than an optimized randomization theory. Temporal only.
Steady-state ATE, bias and variance analysis, asymptotic inference/power/coverage
(Appendix A). Read intro/related pp1-3, conclusion p8; narrow assumptions p3.

Assumption3 (Mixing time), p3, verbatim:
"For each kappa in {0,1}, the CTMC is geometrically ergodic: there exist C >= 1
and lambda_kappa > 0 such that for all x in X and t >= 0,"
  ||P_kappa(x,. ;t)-pi_kappa||_TV <= C e^(-lambda_kappa t).
lambda_min=min(lambda_0,lambda_1). Infinite decaying carryover, stationary
continuous-time real-valued state process. Other structural assumptions crucial:
Assumption1 stochastic monotonicity of each kernel in initial state;
Assumption2 P_0 stochastically dominated by P_1;
Assumption4 bounded nondecreasing rewards r_kappa with r_0(x)<=r_1(x).
Target tau=pi_1 r_1-pi_0 r_0. Design alternates deterministically at intervals l.
Known values: above lambda/C are existence bounds for analysis, not a requirement
of conventional DM/HW computation. OU specialization has mean-reversion theta,
and theta*l controls effective convergence. For practical illustration theta,
mean and noise are estimated by likelihood; do not say lambda is known.
Authors: DM underestimates stationary effect under structural conditions; HW can
overestimate variance in OU example; washout reduces bias at data cost. Conclusion
p8 proposes conventional first-stage inference only when treatment is expected
to obey these conditions, and advanced debiasing if inconclusive.
Reviewer's assessment: conservatism is conditional, not universal across switchback
outcome processes. Downward point-estimate bias plus inflated variance does not
by itself establish valid two-sided GATE coverage for arbitrary carryover. Need
distinguish stationary ATE vs transient effect. State exists in model but DM need
not estimate full state. OU illustration relaxes general bounded reward condition.

6. GLYNN, JOHARI AND RASOULI (FOUNDATION)
Peter Glynn, Ramesh Johari, Mohammad Rasouli, "Adaptive Experimental Design with
Temporal Interference: A Maximum Likelihood Approach". Local arXiv:2006.05591v2
[stat.ME], 24 Dec2022, first page says Preprint Under review; original 2020 work
(cite original publication year if bibliography metadata confirmed separately).
Scope: BOTH adaptive design and estimation, broader than fixed-block switchback;
temporal. Long-run stationary reward contrast alpha=alpha(2)-alpha(1), MLE
consistency, asymptotic variance/CLTs, not primary sequential hypothesis testing.
Read intro/related pp1-2 and conclusion pp8-9; setup pp2-3 narrow lookup.

Exact structural assumptions p2: "We assume a finite state space S." and
"We assume both P^(1) and P^(2) are irreducible." Unknown stationary transition
matrices on common observable state. Rewards bounded, conditionally independent
of past given state/action/next state. No fixed carryover window, no numerical
mixing rate required by these statements. Irreducibility alone does not imply
aperiodic total-variation convergence; do not equate this to Hu-Wager contraction.
TAR policy condition requires limiting positive state-action sampling frequencies
(Definition1 pp4-5). MLE estimates transitions and reward means; efficient design
learns unknown primitives online with forced exploration.
Authors explicitly introduction p2: one run, "without prior knowledge of any of
the parameters of either policy or their rewards." No known m/t_mix is required.
Full current state/state-space knowledge is needed for action selection and MLE;
conclusion p9 acknowledges full state knowledge may be infeasible. Authors focus
on asymptotic efficiency, leaving finite-horizon tuning as future work.
Reviewer's assessment: an important alternative tradeoff: replace exact horizon
knowledge with observed stationary finite-state model and enough exploration;
should not criticize as requiring known mixing just because chain dependence exists.

7. LI ET AL. (CONGESTION)
Shuangning Li, Ramesh Johari, Xu Kuang, Stefan Wager, "Experimenting Under
Stochastic Congestion". arXiv:2302.12093v6 [eess.SY], 12 Aug2026; title page
August13 2026. Footnote explicitly requests citation "S. Li, R. Johari, X. Kuang
and S. Wager" because Kuang published under another full name previously.
Scope: BOTH analysis and alternatives in design; broader congestion interference,
not geographical graph spillover. Temporal dynamic customer-to-customer effects.
Estimand is local total policy gradient V'(p) of stationary throughput, not just
finite ATE between arbitrary distant policies. CLTs, variance estimators and CIs.
Read intro/litreview pp1-5, implications pp22-23; narrow assumptions pp6,9-10.

Model: continuous-time birth-death queue, state-dependent accepted Poisson arrivals
lambda_k(p), independent exponential service times mean1/mu, FCFS and no preemption.
Assumption1 (Bounded queue length), p6, verbatim:
"There exists a constant K in N_+ such that for any price p,
K = min{k: lambda_k(p)=0}."
Assumption3 p10: positive B0,B1,B2 with B0<=lambda_k(p)<=B1 and
|lambda_k''(p)|<=B2 for all relevant p,k<K.
Assumption4 (Interval length), p10, verbatim:
"In the interval switchback experiment, the interval length l_T satisfies
l_T/T -> 0 and l_T/sqrt(T) -> infinity as T -> infinity."
No finite carryover cutoff: stationary finite irreducible queue has ongoing mixing;
increasing block lengths control boundary bias rather than assume exact washout.
Assumption2 p9: sqrt(T)*zeta_T -> infinity and sqrt(T)*zeta_T^2 ->0, ensuring
enough price signal but small finite-difference bias. Nonstationary extension
assumes finitely many regimes/change points (Assumption5), not arbitrary variation.
Known values/observations: weighted direct-effect estimator uses observed queue
length/state-specific arrival counts and times, empirical occupancy; no need to
infer full model primitives first. Idle-time formula p8 explicitly multiplies by
service rate mu; reviewed front matter does not settle known vs estimated mu, so
do not claim all estimators eliminate mu. Queue capacity structural bound and
reference price/perturbations chosen; no numerical mixing time input specified.
Authors: weighted direct-effect estimator no worse asymptotic variance than standard
switchback estimator under model; user-level local price randomization also works,
avoiding fixed block precommitment. Idle-time method needs milder work-conserving
structure. Implications explicitly acknowledge model misspecification concern.
Reviewer's assessment: local policy-gradient design/inference, not generic global
effect solution; structural queue model supplies information otherwise unavailable.

8. SUN ET AL. (ARMA)
Ke Sun, Linglong Kong, Hongtu Zhu, Chengchun Shi. Exact local title is
"ARMA-Design: Optimal Treatment Allocation Strategies for A/B Testing in
Partially Observable Experiments" (file name adds Time Series, not on title page).
arXiv:2408.05342v4 [econ.EM], 11 Jan2025. File name2024 reflects initial version.
Scope: BOTH; temporal only, spatial extensions discussed as future work pp36-37.
Steady-state all+1 vs all-1 ATE; MSE-optimal allocation, estimators and inference
discussion, not primarily randomization hypothesis testing.
Read intro/related pp2-9 and discussion pp36-39; narrow model/assumptions pp12-17,
design nuisance estimation p24.

Controlled ARMA(p,q), equation(3.2), p12:
  Y_t=mu+sum_{j=1}^p a_j Y_{t-j}+b U_t+sum_{j=0}^q theta_j epsilon_{t-j},
theta_0=1; epsilon_t i.i.d. mean0 variance sigma^2 (p13).
Assumption1 (No unit root), pp14-15, verbatim:
"All the roots of the polynomial 1-sum_{j=1}^p a_j y^j lie outside the unit circle."
VARMA version Assumption2 p21 puts roots of det(I-sum A_j y^j) outside circle.
Carryover can persist infinitely through stable AR recursion; finite AR order p
is not an exact finite carryover window. MA(q) models dependence/partial observability,
and determines how much action-history dependence optimal observation-agnostic
design needs. Target ATE=2b/(1-sum a_j); vector form2e'(I-A)^-1 b.
Small-signal asymptotics pp16-17: T->infinity AND |ATE|->0 (arbitrary rate);
not a numerical known constant, rather an approximation regime.
Known vs learned: p24 explicitly says coefficients c_k and p,q depend on true
model "which are typically unknown"; proposes historical estimation and AIC/BIC,
or sequential daily updating. Thus p,q/coefficient knowledge NOT assumed given
for practical algorithm; correctness/adequacy of fitted stable linear model remains
a burden. Discussion p38 says optimality not guaranteed when small-signal fails;
model-based design robust empirically under tested misspecifications, no universal
robustness theorem should be attributed. Seasonal filtering used for stationarity.
Reviewer's assessment: partial observability supported within linear stable model;
does not solve arbitrary unknown latent nonlinear carryover or nonstationarity.

9. WU ET AL. (TRANSFORMER RL)
Xiangkun Wu, Qianglin Wen, Yingying Zhang, Hongtu Zhu, Ting Li, Chengchun Shi,
"Designing Time Series Experiments in A/B Testing with Transformer Reinforcement
Learning". ICLR2026; local arXiv:2602.01853v1 [cs.LG], 2 Feb2026.
Scope: DESIGN ONLY in primary contribution (takes an ATE estimation procedure as
input, though computes estimators for optimization); temporal only, multiple-unit
spillovers explicitly future work p12 conclusion. Causal finite-horizon ATE MSE,
not newly developed hypothesis-test/CI guarantee.
Read intro/related pp1-6 and conclusion p12; narrow setup/design assumption pp6-8.

General conditional law p6:
P_t(Y,O|H_{t-1},O_t,A_t)=Pr(Y_t in Y,O_{t+1} in O|H_{t-1},O_t,A_t),
where H_{t-1} is entire observed past. No exact finite carryover horizon, no required
Markov sufficiency of current feature vector, no prescribed geometric decay.
Theorem1 p6 states an EXISTENCE impossibility result: for DR estimator there exist
DGPs whose unique variance-optimal allocation uses entire history. It is not a
universal proof that every fixed-memory policy is suboptimal in every DGP.
Actual operational assumption p7, verbatim:
"We assume access to a simulation environment that can generate (O_t,A_t,Y_t)_t
that approximates the experimental data generating process".
RL reward is discounted negative squared difference from simulator Monte Carlo
all-treatment/all-control ATE. Authors p8 propose learning simulator from accrued
data if none initially available, updating design daily.
Known values: no known m/t_mix/AR order, but requires credible counterfactual
simulation/estimated environment, chosen estimator and neural/training settings.
Observed histories used; no demand for observing latent physical full state.
Reviewer's assessment: relaxes explicit low-dimensional carryover restrictions by
moving burden to simulator fidelity, training data and approximation; "without
restrictive assumptions" is an author positioning statement, not literal absence
of requirements. DR efficiency assumes regularity and adequate nuisance learning;
empirical design gains do not alone certify valid CIs under simulation error.

10. FARIAS ET AL. (DQ)
Vivek F. Farias, Andrew A. Li, Tianyi Peng, Andrew Zheng,
"Markovian Interference in Experiments". arXiv:2206.02371v2 [cs.LG], 9 Jun2022.
Scope: ANALYSIS ONLY under given simple Bernoulli randomization (explicitly
eschews design as lever); temporal Markovian cross-customer interference, not graph
geographical spillovers. Steady-state ATE, bias-variance, asymptotic normality;
not an exact unbiased GATE inference solution.
Read intro/related pp2-9 and discussion/conclusion pp21-25; narrow mixing pp11-12.

Stationary MDP with unknown transition p(s,a,s'), observed state-action-reward
trajectory; experiment pi_1/2 produces P_1/2=(P_0+P_1)/2. Assumption1 p11-12:
"There exist constants C and lambda such that for all s in S,"
  d_TV(P_1/2^k(s,.),rho_1/2)<=C lambda^k.
For meaningful geometric mixing, lambda<1. This is a mixing property of the
EXPERIMENTATION chain, distinct from uniform per-policy/time contraction in Hu.
Theorem1 condition p12:
  d_TV(p(s,1,.),p(s,0,.))<=delta for every state.
DQ bias bounded C' r_max delta^2/(1-lambda)^2. Small transition perturbation is
not identical to short memory. No exact cutoff; Markovian dependence can persist.
Known values: C,lambda,delta bound accuracy, not stated known inputs to DQ.
Full state observed and Q_pi1/2 must be estimated, or approximated. Authors obtain
second-order bias, potentially exponentially smaller variance than unbiased OPE;
asymptotic normality does not erase approximation bias in target ATE. Discussion
considers higher-order corrections and regularization tradeoff, not proof of
unqualified exact inference with fixed first-order estimator.
Reviewer's assessment: useful broader foundation for model-based precision;
should distinguish approximate policy-gradient effect from exact rollout contrast.

11. SHI ET AL. (DYNAMIC CAUSAL EFFECTS)
Chengchun Shi, Xiaoyu Wang, Shikai Luo, Hongtu Zhu, Jieping Ye, Rui Song,
"Dynamic Causal Effects Evaluation in A/B Testing with a Reinforcement Learning
Framework". Local arXiv:2002.01711v6 [cs.LG], 3 Nov2022; file labeled2023
(published year may be2023, confirm independently if using journal metadata).
Scope: ANALYSIS ONLY of a variety of given Markov, alternating, adaptive designs;
temporal only. Explicit causal estimands plus sequential hypothesis testing for
H0: integrated value contrast<=0 vs H1>0; variance/uncertainty theory enabling
monitoring. Broader RL foundation rather than switchback design paper.
Read intro/related pp2-7 and discussion pp28-32; narrow model pp9-10,
technical geometric assumption C1 p46 only for exact copying.

MA / CMIA observed-data equations(1),(2), p10:
  Pr(S_{t+1} in B|A_t,S_t,{S_j,A_j,Y_j}_{j<t})=P(B;A_t,S_t),
  E(Y_t|A_t,S_t,{S_j,A_j,Y_j}_{j<t})=r(A_t,S_t).
Consistency and sequential randomization (CA,SRA) required. Time-homogeneous
P,r and fully recorded sufficient state; past action influence must pass through
state. Authors: "the state variables shall be chosen to include those that serve
as important mediators between past treatments and current outcomes" (p10).
No finite exact cutoff. Discount 0<gamma<1 in estimand weights outcomes; gamma
is chosen preference, NOT physical carryover-decay rate.
For asymptotic inference C1(ii) p46:
  ||Pr(S_t in .|S_0=s)-Pi(.)||_TV<=M(s)rho^t,
rho<1 and integral M(s)Pi(ds)<infinity; C1(i) transition density bounded away
from0 and infinity. Alternating design uses analogous even/odd subsequence
ergodicity (C1iii), adaptive designs additional limiting behavior-policy conditions.
Known values: no known mixing numerical constant required in stated test; analyst
chooses discount gamma, reference initial-state distribution G, function basis,
alpha-spending schedule; learns value-function nuisance. Appropriate observed
state sufficiency is substantive, not an empirical certainty.
Estimand p8-9 V(a;s)=sum_{t>=0} gamma^t E[Y_t^*(always a)|S0=s],
tau=integral(V(1;s)-V(0;s))G(ds). It differs from stationary average reward or
finite-horizon GATE. Reviewer's assessment: not interchangeable effect target;
full-state/stationarity assumptions buy sequential valid testing under studied
regularity and allocation mechanisms.

12. JOHARI, PENG AND XING (TPG)
Ramesh Johari, Tianyi Peng, Wenqian Xing, "Estimation of Treatment Effects Under
Nonstationarity via the Truncated Policy Gradient Estimator".
arXiv:2506.05308v3 [stat.ME], 2 Jun2026; title page June3 2026.
Scope: ANALYSIS ONLY under Bernoulli assignment (explicit alternative to careful
switchback tuning); temporal dynamic interference, no geographic graph.
Finite-horizon GATE approximation, bias-variance, CLT/HAC CIs; caveat on centering
below. Read intro/related pp1-4 and conclusion p18; narrow assumptions pp5,13.

Assumption1 (Mixing time), p5, verbatim:
"There exists a constant gamma, 0 < gamma < 1, such that, for any time t in [T],
any action z in Z, and any pair of distributions f and f' over the state space X,
the following holds"
  ||f'P_z^t-fP_z^t||_TV <= gamma||f'-f||_TV.
Finite state, unknown nonstationary kernels, bounded reward; no exact cutoff;
uniform geometric contraction. Only rewards/assignments used, no state knowledge
needed for estimator. Truncation k is an estimator horizon, not assumed true last lag.
Theorem1 p8 defines delta=sup_{t,x}||P_1^t(x,.)-P_0^t(x,.)||_TV and gives bias
  O(((1-gamma^k)/(1-gamma)^2)*delta^2*M + (gamma^k/(1-gamma))*delta*M).
The first term is Taylor approximation; increasing k cannot generally remove it.
Known values: gamma/delta are analysis constants, not explicitly known estimator
inputs. Empirical k selection heuristic post experiment, no exact unknown-tail
certification. Advantage in bounds is especially when delta<1-gamma (p9).

INFERENCE CAVEAT (p13):
Assumption2: sigma_k^2=lim_T T^-1 Var(sum_t B_t) in (0,infinity).
Assumption3 (Cesaro-L2 mean stability), exact equation:
  Delta_T^2=T^-1 sum_t(mu_t-mubar_T)^2=o(T^-2/3), mu_t=E[B_t].
Thus arbitrary nonstationarity in error bounds is restricted for feasible HAC
inference. Theorem2 fixes finite k and states
  sqrt(T)(hat_tau_k-E[hat_tau_k]) => N(0,sigma_k^2),
NOT sqrt(T)(hat_tau_k-GATE). HAC consistently estimates variance under Assumptions
1-3 and growing bandwidth O(T^1/3). Reviewer's assessment: ordinary intervals
based on this CLT cover mean estimator target; GATE coverage additionally needs
negligible residual bias or valid bias allowance. Authors introduction/conclusion
describe valid inference, but review must retain exact centering caveat rather
than claiming bias-free fixed-k inference for general GATE.

CROSS-PAPER SYNTHESIS FOR REVIEW
* Finite m, geometric contraction, stable linear recursion, and Markov sufficient
  state are substantively different assumptions; compare separately.
* Strong finite-m guarantee needs valid bound in actual exposure rule. An existence
  mixing bound only becomes numerical prior knowledge when a particular tuning
  prescription uses it; do not equate all geometric assumptions to known t_mix.
* Glynn learns stationary finite-state primitives without known m/t_mix; Sun learns
  orders/coefs from history; Wen estimates RL nuisances and assesses carryover/
  autocorrelation; Wu moves restriction burden to simulator. These are different
  learning targets, not uniformly "known carryover."
* Whole-system temporal randomization addresses contemporaneous spatial
  interactions by joint assignment but does not mean paper models geographic
  spillovers. Jia explicitly jointly models graph spillover and temporal decay.
* ATE denotes different quantities: finite-horizon global effect (Hu spectral,
  Jia, Wen, Wu, Johari); steady-state contrast (Glynn, Blanchet, Farias, Sun);
  discounted value contrast (Shi); derivative/local policy gradient (Li).
* Estimation MSE, conditional asymptotic CLT, and exact sharp-null testing are
  distinct. Johari/DQ approximation bias especially precludes silently translating
  asymptotic normality into confidence coverage of exact GATE.
